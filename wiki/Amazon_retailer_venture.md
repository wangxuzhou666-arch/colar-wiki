From June 2023 to May 2025, in parallel with his undergraduate studies
at the [[University of Nottingham]], [[Colar Wang]] co-founded and
operated a cross-border e-commerce venture on the **Amazon**
marketplace, based in London, United Kingdom.

Wang has retrospectively characterized the venture as his *"pre-agent
period"* — an early, informal experiment in using language models and
statistical pipelines to automate decisions that would conventionally
require human operators. In Wang's telling, the venture was formative
less for its commercial outcomes than for the engineering instincts it
established: a bias toward closed-loop systems, statistical
decision-making, and the explicit modeling of operational uncertainty.

## Work

### Programmatic advertising

On the growth side, Wang built a **closed-loop programmatic
advertising framework** integrated with the Amazon Ads API. The system
ingested granular keyword-level metrics, clustered search queries
semantically, and used A/B testing to dynamically reallocate budget
toward high-conversion long-tail terms. Internal records attributed a
**20 percent improvement in return on ad spend (ROAS)** to the system.

### Market-intelligence pipeline

On the discovery side, Wang engineered a **market-intelligence
pipeline** that applied natural-language processing over unstructured
review and listing data to surface high-potential demand gaps. The
pipeline modeled the relationship between sales volume and review
distribution to identify underserved high-GMV segments, and its output
informed the venture's product roadmap.

Wang has cited this pipeline as a direct intellectual precursor to
the [[First-principles_thinking|first-principles, intent-oriented]]
agent product research he has pursued since.

### Unit economics engine

Wang also designed a dynamic **"total landed cost"** engine that
governed pricing and margin thresholds, incorporating stochastic
demand forecasting into working-capital allocation. The system was
credited internally with accelerating inventory turnover by
approximately 15 percent.

## Legacy

Wang has described the Amazon venture as the period in which he
"first discovered that the hard problem of commerce is not selling,
but deciding." He has identified the NLP market-intelligence pipeline,
in particular, as the experience that convinced him language models
could be end-to-end decision systems rather than merely
retrieval-over-documents — an observation that subsequently shaped
both [[KitchenSurvivor]] and [[AgentConfig]].
